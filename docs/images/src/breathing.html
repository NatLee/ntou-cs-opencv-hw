Counting breaths NecklineDetection · two versions

Breathing lifts the chest and shoulders a little. Both programs find where the neck meets the collar and watch that line move.

Schematic of the skin mask: skin is light, everything else dark. Both programs need a webcam, so this is drawn from the code, not captured.
1
Mirror and find skin
YCrCb colour range Cr 137–177, Cb 77–127, then erode and dilate to remove specks. Trackbars in the Control window tune the range.
2
Find the base of the neck
Scan rows from the bottom and columns from the right for the first run of 120 skin pixels in a row: the widest part of the neck.
3
Trace the collar
Along that run, look up to 50 px down for where skin stops. That edge is the neckline.
4
Turn movement into breaths
Follow the row frame by frame. When it changes direction after moving more than 5 px, that is half a breath.
NecklineDetectionUsingNormalMethod
Paints the neckline blue in the Original window, shows the mask in Skin and prints a line once per full breath, starting from 0.

NecklineDetectionUsingOpticalFlow
Works on a half-size frame and keeps only the largest skin area. It draws Farneback optical-flow arrows along the collar in the flow window and prints the direction when the collar moves more than 7 px.
